How to read RPE — read this first

RPE = how many reps you have left in the tank when you stop a set. You're aiming for RPE 8 on most lifts, which means you stop with about two good reps still in you. You do not train to failure on a cut, it costs recovery you can't spare.

Worked example — Back Squat

The Lower A card says 4 × 4–6. So four working sets of four to six reps. Warm up (empty bar, then 60kg ×5, 80kg ×3), then do your four sets at 100kg.

  • The last set is your gauge. Rack it and ask how many more you could've done.
  • About 2 more = RPE 8 = perfect, you've got the weight right.
  • 4+ more left and it flew up = too light → go to 105kg next time.
  • Couldn't hit 4 reps, or the last rep was an all-out grind = too heavy → drop ~5kg.

Progression — "build from here"

Double progression: once you can hit the top of the rep range on every set (all four sets of six) while the last set is still only RPE 8, add the smallest jump (2.5kg each side, +5kg total), then drop back to the bottom of the range and climb again. On a cut you might only nudge these up a little across 12 weeks, and that's a win. Log every set.

Deloads — weeks 5 and 9

You can't run hard for 12 straight weeks in a deficit. On those weeks cut sets by ~40%, drop loads ~10%, keep everything at RPE 5–6, and pair each with a refeed day. If the elbow niggles before then, deload the pressing early. Never train through tendon pain.

Cardio & Recovery

Steps are the engine, low fatigue and muscle-sparing. No HIIT, it steals recovery from your legs and you can't afford that in a deficit. The food creates the deficit; cardio is the dial you turn only if the scale stalls. And recovery is where most cuts are quietly lost.

Cardio & Steps

Daily

  • 10,000–12,000 steps every day. You already hit ~9.7k on a quiet day.
  • Desk job kills this. Walk your calls, take stairs, a 15-min lunch walk banks ~2k and clears your head.

Structured · Wed + Sat

  • 2 × 25–30 min incline treadmill walk or easy bike, conversational pace.
  • Stall-breaker only: if the 7-day average flatlines for 2 weeks, add a third session before you cut food again.

Recovery

Sleep — non-negotiable

  • 7.5–8 hours, fixed wake time even at weekends.
  • Last caffeine by early afternoon. Keep any pre-gym hit small or it wrecks sleep.
  • Poor sleep is the single biggest driver of the next-day blowout. Protect it like a session.

Supplements — minimal, proven

  • Creatine 5g daily, no loading. Best muscle-retention tool on a cut.
  • Vitamin D3, sensible at Irish latitude with indoor work.
  • Fish oil for joints and the elbow.
  • Check halal capsules. Skip fat burners, they're a waste of money.

Hydration

  • 3 litres+ daily. High protein and creatine both need it.
  • Hunger is often thirst. A glass of water before you decide you're hungry saves a lot of snacking.

Daily shape

  • Pre-log the day each morning, copy yesterday.
  • Train evening with Mufleh, the fixed slot is your accountability.
  • Walk at lunch, wind down screens before bed.

The Lock-In System

Your block was never knowledge, it was execution drifting once the novelty fades. So this runs on systems and accountability, not motivation. Motivation will be gone by week three. These won't be.

  1. Bolt it to the gym anchor. You never skip Mufleh. Tell him the target and send him your Sunday weigh-in average every week. Make the diet as visible and accountable as the lifting.
  2. Sunday checkpoint, 10 minutes. Log the 7-day weight average, take one progress photo in the same light, adjust food only if the rules say so. That ritual is the whole control system.
  3. Pre-commit to the stall now. Around week 4 to 6 the scale freezes for a week or two while fat still leaves. Your response is decided today: do nothing, hold the line. You can't renegotiate a decision you already made when thinking clearly.
  4. Judge by data, not the mirror. Trunk fat empties last, so your abs lag the scale by weeks. The face and arms lean out first. Trust the average.

The Scale Rules

  • Weigh daily, morning, after the toilet, before food.
  • Act only on the 7-day average, never a single number.
  • Losing faster than 0.8 kg/wk? Add 150 kcal, that pace eats muscle at your leanness.
  • Slower than 0.4 kg/wk after 2 weeks? Cut 150 kcal or add a cardio session.
  • Ignore the scale for 2–3 days after any high day. That's water and glycogen, not fat.

The 12-Week Arc

What each phase should feel like, so nothing surprises you into quitting. The hard part is weeks 6 to 8. The system is built to carry you through it.

Weeks 1–4 · Establish
Lock the routine. Expect ~0.6–0.7kg/wk and a first “whoosh” after a flat patch. ~90kg by end of week 4.
Week 5 · Deload + refeed
Back off training, one refeed day. You'll look and feel fuller, that's glycogen, not fat. Don't panic.
Weeks 6–8 · The grind
Novelty's gone, this is where the system earns its keep. Scale may stall, hold. Arms and face visibly leaner. ~87kg.
Week 9 · Deload + re-scan
Second deload. Re-Tanita to confirm fat is leaving and lean is holding, adjust calories off real data.
Weeks 10–12 · Final push
Tighten to ~84–85kg / 12%. Top abs visible, definition arriving. Late-August target hit.
After · Decide
Hold the leanness, or push to ~10% for a full shred into September. New scan, new plan.
